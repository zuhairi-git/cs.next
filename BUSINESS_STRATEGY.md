# CoreScene: a business for the next chapter

This is a proposed operating model, implemented in the website for review. Prices are hypotheses, not validated demand or revenue forecasts.

## The business shift

From selling pages on HubSpot, WordPress, and Wix to implementing and improving connected customer journeys for small service businesses. The website remains an entry point; the deliverable is a working system that connects enquiry, customer context, and follow-through.

AI makes production faster. CoreScene earns its fee through diagnosing the right problem, understanding the customer's business, connecting systems, checking real cases, and making the result usable by the team. Use existing products when they solve the problem; do not build a proprietary agent platform as the first move.

## Start with a narrow buyer

Initial hypothesis: owner-led B2B service firms with roughly 5–30 people, an existing website, some inbound demand, and a CRM or shared intake process. Start in the existing Finnish network and work in the language the team can support well. Examples include consultancies and specialist agencies with enquiry-to-proposal handoffs.

Qualify for a repeated, observable problem: enquiries without an owner, copied information across tools, or slow intake. Seek an accountable internal owner and access to a baseline. A company without an offer or demand needs a different engagement first. Do not sell automation as a substitute for finding customers.

## A three-stage commercial model

| Offer | Proposed price | Useful output | Scope boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Signal | EUR 750, fixed scope | Journey review, opportunity map, one implementation brief, baseline measure | Diagnostic only; no production integrations |
| Launch | From EUR 4,500 | One customer journey: website/landing flow, CRM connection, one priority workflow, tests and handover | Agree pages, connections, data migration, AI behaviour, and acceptance criteria in the proposal |
| Orbit | From EUR 650/month | Monthly system review, answer checks where AI is used, one prioritised improvement cycle, report | Define a capacity cap, response expectations, and out-of-scope work in the agreement |

The diagnostic must be valuable even if the client does not buy implementation. Orbit is optional, not an artificial maintenance dependency. Retainers pay for improvement and operational ownership, not generic access to AI. Third-party subscriptions and usage are separate. Website pricing is explicitly indicative and excludes applicable VAT.

Before committing to these prices, estimate delivery, project management, revisions, tooling, acquisition costs, and contingency. As a capacity check, 750/6 hours = EUR 125/hour, 4,500/36 hours = EUR 125/hour, and 650/5 hours = EUR 130/hour, all before costs. These are planning envelopes, not promised margins. Narrow scope or change the quote when the work exceeds them. No unlimited changes or unlimited support.

## A first repeatable implementation

Visitor enquiry -> structured capture -> CRM contact and owner -> draft reply from approved material -> human review -> follow-up task -> simple reporting.

Use deterministic automation for moving records and assigning tasks. Use AI only for ambiguity, summarising, or drafting where the result can be checked. Agree knowledge sources, access, uncertainty handling, retries, duplicate prevention, and escalation. Test missing fields, incomplete information, failed connections, and inappropriate draft answers. Record who owns the system after handover.

## Validate before expanding

1. Interview 8–10 existing or warm prospects. Map the last real enquiry, time spent, failures, software, and buying responsibility. Do not pitch a general AI transformation.
2. Offer a small number of paid Signal engagements. Record objections, delivery hours, and which bottlenecks repeat.
3. Build the first tightly scoped Launch projects using the client's existing accounts. Capture baseline and follow-up measures with permission.
4. Offer Orbit only where a recurring backlog and owner exist. Review whether improvement work fits the capacity cap.
5. Publish case studies only after real results exist and the client approves them. No invented logos, metrics, testimonials, or revenue guarantees.

Choose one business measure per project: time to first response, share of enquiries with an owner, time per intake, or qualified-enquiry conversion. Track technical reliability and correction rate alongside it. Traffic and lead quality can change, so avoid attributing every change to the implementation.

## Positioning and launch choices

Use the line: “We connect your website, CRM, and everyday workflows into a system that helps your business move forward.” Keep the cinematic identity as a memorable wrapper around clear language. Interstellar is the default Epic theme; Dune adds an alternative world without changing the commercial offer.

Sales motion: use existing relationships, a practical diagnostic, a scoped implementation proposal, and an optional improvement partnership. This document does not authorize outreach or publishing. Validate the niche, delivery capacity, and prices before treating the model as established.

## Evidence behind the direction

Reviewed 16 October 2026:

- [Wix AI Website Builder](https://www.wix.com/ai-website-builder): Wix offers conversational site creation. Inference: page production alone is a weaker differentiator for an agency.
- [HubSpot: Understand Breeze](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/ai/understand-breeze): the platform includes AI assistance and agents using configured knowledge and tools. Inference: integration, useful configuration, adoption, and ongoing review are services CoreScene can test without inventing a new platform.
- [HubSpot Customer Agent partner resources](https://offers.hubspot.com/customer-agent-resource-hub-for-partners): describes business-context training and handoff to human representatives. This supports evaluating configured workflows, not promising autonomous results.

These sources establish platform capabilities. They do not validate CoreScene's demand, proposed pricing, or outcomes.
